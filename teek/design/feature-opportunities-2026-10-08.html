Make launching feel like bringing a community together.

The proposed Teek experience: create understandable terms, rehearse the outcomes, invite your community into a private opening, then publish a verifiable launch record. Start with creators who already have an audience. That audience is a hypothesis to validate, not established customer demand.

What exists today. A devnet private budget-bid opening on a fixed Meteora DBC curve, atomic pool creation and pooled opening purchase, allocations and refunds. This is not limit-price discovery. Public funding and timing remain visible; closing reveals bids and earlier edits. Maximum 24 registered wallets. Features below are proposals, not released functionality.

Eight features with a clear job

Filter the proposed roadmap. “Core” is the recommended next experience; “Growth” adds retention and distribution; “Protocol” needs new settlement or custody design and review.

Showing all 8 features.

01 · CORE / HIGH PRIORITY

Launch Studio

Creator moment: “Name my token, choose a launch preset, pick a date. Show me what everyone will accept.”

Three steps: identity, economics, review. Generate metadata and an appropriate DBC configuration; show costs, supply, team allocation, fees, timing and refund conditions before signing.

Why choose it: Turns a protocol configuration task into a launch people can understand.

Preset generation and transaction orchestration are real work. Keep advanced settings accessible. AI may draft copy, but must not silently choose financial terms.

02 · CORE / HIGH PRIORITY

Launch Rehearsal

Creator moment: “Let me see a weak opening, a full opening and an oversubscribed opening before I publish.”

Run deterministic scenarios against the selected public curve, including fees, output floors, rounding and failed-condition refunds. Invite test participants into a sandbox.

Why choose it: Creators and buyers learn the mechanism through their own launch, not documentation.

Scenarios are hypothetical, not demand or future-price forecasts. Match exact deployed program rules before using estimates in the real signing flow.

03 · CORE / HIGH PRIORITY

Community Launch Rooms

Creator moment: “My Telegram group has one link, one countdown and one opening to join.”

A branded mobile room with creator updates, clear terms, personal participation status and opt-in reminders. Embed the same room in a creator site; add sponsored transaction fees after the wallet flow is proven.

Why choose it: The creator brings their audience directly into the launch, with less explanation and fewer abandoned steps.

The current protocol has separate funding and private bidding phases. Do not promise one-click participation across them. Public deposits are not sealed demand; never show private bid totals or inferred “you are in range” hints.

04 · CORE / HIGH PRIORITY

Launch Passport

Buyer moment: “Show me what is fixed, what the creator can change and what actually happened.”

Before opening: a readable record of configuration, authorities, token allocation, fees, privacy boundaries and refund terms. After settlement: actual accepted funds, output, shared opening price, claims and refunds with source transactions.

Why choose it: Gives creators a shareable explanation and buyers a way to check it. Export JSON for wallets and launchpad partners.

A verified fact is not a safe-token certification. No bot accusations from wallet counts; no promise of profits or identity-based fairness.

05 · PROTOCOL EXTENSION

Creator Commitment Packages

Creator moment: “I want everyone to know exactly when our tokens unlock and where our earned fees go.”

Choose disclosed team vesting and liquidity policies. Optionally route creator fees into a time-locked operating budget, with recipients and powers recorded in the Passport.

Why choose it: Serious creators can make concrete commitments without assembling several tools themselves.

Meteora already supports configurations for vesting, fees and LP ownership. Show which are enforced natively and which require Teek contracts. Opening-purchase funds cannot also fund a project treasury; a treasury raise is a separate architecture. Subjective milestones need an explicit human dispute process.

06 · PROTOCOL EXTENSION

Reward the People Who Built the Community

Creator moment: “Our designer, moderators and early testers should have defined rewards, not vague promises.”

Publish a fixed contributor allocation and eligibility rules before launch. Support an approved contributor list and a separate vesting/claim path; allow creator-fee shares where the selected infrastructure supports them.

Why choose it: Makes launching a collaboration with visible responsibilities and rewards.

Fee sharing already exists elsewhere. The proposed value is contribution workflow plus transparent opening terms. Do not take undisclosed supply from buyers or reward fabricated accounts and trading volume. Human-approved eligibility is not permissionless proof of personhood.

07 · GROWTH / RETENTION

Creator Mission Control

Creator moment: “The launch finished. Tell me what needs attention this week.”

One dashboard for fee claims, pool/migration state, upcoming unlocks, creator updates and opt-in alerts. Keep a factual history of launches, settled outcomes and refund completion.

Why choose it: Gives a reason to return after token creation, and makes repeat launching easier.

Record confirmed chain events. Graduation has not been demonstrated by current Teek demo pools. Price and volume are not measures of creator integrity; successful refund handling should remain visible.

08 · GROWTH / DISTRIBUTION

Teek Inside Other Launchpads

Operator moment: “Add private community openings to our platform without rebuilding the settlement engine.”

Provide a branded room, typed integration client, shared receipts and disclosed partner economics. Start with one partner workflow rather than a universal launch SDK.

Why choose it: Existing communities and launchpad operators can distribute Teek while preserving their own brand.

Proposed business model, not proven demand or contracted revenue. Requires production capacity and recovery guarantees; the current 24-wallet prototype cannot serve a mass launch unchanged.

Recommended product sequence

DraftRehearsePublish termsInvite communityFundEdit private bidSettle / refundInspect receiptManage launch

Build first: Studio + Rehearsal + Launch Room + Passport. Treat these as one complete experience, not four competing projects. Validate with creators who already have an audience and an actual upcoming launch.

Test the adoption hypothesis: Can a new creator produce a correct draft without help? Can a participant explain failure, oversubscription and privacy after reviewing the preview? How many invited supporters finish both funding and bidding? Can a creator independently explain the final receipt? Ask whether a real launch would move to Teek, and what prevents it.

Before scale: Design beyond 24 wallets, automate the supported lifecycle, improve wallet onboarding, document recovery paths and complete production security review. More features do not remove those requirements.

A failure worth learning from

Rally's creator-token sidechain shut down in January 2023; contemporary reporting described inaccessible creator NFTs. This is an adjacent failed product, not a Teek mechanism competitor. The product lesson is to make assets and recovery usable beyond one company's website. This does not establish why every creator-token project fails. Contemporary shutdown report.
